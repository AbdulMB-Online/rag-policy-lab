# Remote Work Policy v1.0

## RW-01: Purpose and scope

Northstar Analytics supports remote work that maintains reliable service,
collaboration, and protection of company information. This policy applies to
employees assigned to a company office in the United States whose duties can be
performed from an approved home workspace. It governs routine weekly scheduling;
business travel and paid time off follow their respective policies.

Remote work is a work arrangement. Employees remain responsible for their normal
deliverables, scheduled meetings, and working hours. A remote day must not be
recorded as PTO unless the employee is actually taking leave for that time.

The arrangement must identify where work will be performed and how colleagues
can obtain timely support. It is not a substitute for childcare, personal
errands, or another commitment that prevents the employee from performing their
scheduled duties. Employees who need time away should discuss the appropriate
leave process rather than treating remote availability as permission to be
absent. This policy does not approve business travel, change an employee's
assigned office, or establish a right to work from any location with internet
access. Questions involving several policies should be directed to the owners
of the relevant decisions.

## RW-02: Roles and eligibility

Employees may request a recurring arrangement when their duties do not require
continuous physical access to office equipment, secure facilities, or onsite
customer services. Managers assess role suitability and team coverage using the
same criteria for comparable roles. Eligibility does not automatically approve
a particular schedule.

Employees maintain an appropriate workspace and accurate availability calendar.
Managers approve schedules and communicate coverage needs. People Operations
maintains arrangement records and resolves eligibility disputes. IT Security
advises on device access and information protection. Managers must provide a
written reason when declining a request.

Before recommending an arrangement, managers consider customer response needs,
meetings requiring physical attendance, shared equipment, and the availability
of colleagues who cover related duties. The decision should describe specific
operational needs rather than relying on assumptions about remote workers.
Employees should explain how they will meet those needs and identify any known
constraints. A manager may request clarification before making a decision, but
must still record a response within the review period in RW-04. If an employee
disagrees with the assessment, People Operations reviews the documented reasons
and may ask both parties for a practical alternative.

## RW-03: Policy rules

The following requirements apply to every approved recurring arrangement.

### RW-03.1: Weekly allowance

Eligible employees may work remotely up to 3 days per calendar week, subject to
manager approval. The calendar week runs Monday through Sunday. Unused remote
days do not carry forward, and holidays or PTO do not increase the allowance.
Employees must attend required onsite activities even when these fall on a
normally approved remote day.

Approval applies to the weekdays recorded in the arrangement, not to any days
the employee chooses later. A cancelled remote day is not a credit for a future
week. When an onsite meeting replaces a scheduled remote day, the employee may
ask to move the remote day within the same calendar week using RW-04 or the
urgent-change process in RW-05. Any replacement remains subject to the weekly
allowance and coverage requirements. A holiday does not require the employee to
perform work, but it also does not create an extra remote entitlement on another
scheduled working day.

### RW-03.2: Availability and workspace

Employees must be reachable through company messaging during their scheduled
working hours and attend team collaboration sessions between 10:00 a.m. and
3:00 p.m. in their assigned office's time zone when those sessions fall within
their working hours. Managers document any agreed schedule variation.

- Use a company-managed device and approved access methods under the Information
  Security Policy.
- Work from the approved US home address with reliable connectivity and a private
  space for confidential discussions.
- Keep company information inaccessible to household members and visitors.

Remote work approval does not authorize working from another country or using
personal devices for company data.

Employees should keep their calendar and messaging status accurate enough for
colleagues to distinguish meetings, focused work, and approved time away. A
short delay while attending a scheduled meeting is different from being
unavailable for an unreported absence. Managers and employees should agree on
reasonable communication expectations for their responsibilities. Confidential
calls require a private setting; headphones alone are insufficient if others
can view protected material. Access problems must be raised with IT support,
and employees must not solve connectivity problems by forwarding company files
to a personal account or switching to an unmanaged device.

## RW-04: Procedures

Use the following process to request or change a recurring arrangement:

1. Submit a request to the manager at least five business days before the proposed
   start date. Include the home work location, requested weekdays, working hours,
   and coverage plan.
2. The manager reviews eligibility, onsite obligations, and coverage, then records
   approval or a reason for declining within three business days of submission.
3. After approval, send the written decision to People Operations and update the
   shared team calendar before the arrangement begins.
4. Request later schedule or location changes through the same process. An
   unanswered request is not approval; continue the existing approved arrangement
   or office schedule until a decision is recorded.

If connectivity fails, notify the manager promptly and agree on a workable
alternative. Time spent unable to work must be reported accurately; employees
must not silently substitute leave or assume that missed work is excused.

The initial request should explain the recurring pattern in plain language.
List the assigned office, the approved home address sought, any fixed onsite
obligations, and who will cover time-sensitive work when the employee is in a
meeting. Include the proposed communication method for urgent issues. Do not
include passwords, customer datasets, private medical information, or unrelated
household details in the request. People Operations may coordinate a separate
discussion if a sensitive personal circumstance needs consideration.

The written decision should state the start date, approved weekdays, working
hours, and any review conditions. Employees should retain the decision in the
approved company system so the manager and People Operations can locate the
same record. A shared calendar helps coordinate attendance but does not replace
that approval record. If the calendar and written decision differ, contact the
manager to resolve the discrepancy before relying on the calendar entry.

For an ordinary change, submit the replacement arrangement at least five
business days before it should begin. Explain what changes and whether the
coverage plan remains workable. Keep the existing arrangement until the new
decision is recorded. For an interruption during a remote day, tell the manager
what work is affected, whether an approved alternative connection is available,
and when another update can be provided. The manager should agree on the next
action, which may include returning to the office when practical. Report any
security concern through SEC-04 immediately rather than waiting for a scheduling
decision.

## RW-05: Exceptions and escalation

For an urgent same-week schedule change, contact the manager before changing
location. The manager may approve different weekdays within the weekly allowance
and must send the decision to People Operations. A request exceeding that
allowance is outside this routine arrangement and requires a separate People
Operations review before any change takes effect.

Escalate unresolved eligibility or scheduling disputes to People Operations.
Report suspected exposure of company information immediately through the incident
reporting process in the Information Security Policy. Remote approval never
waives information protection requirements.

A request for a different address, an unusually extended arrangement, or a
change involving duties that require physical attendance must describe the
specific circumstances. People Operations coordinates any additional review
with the manager and IT Security. A pending review does not authorize the
requested change. Any approved exception must identify its scope and end date,
and must not be interpreted as a permanent change for other employees. Expense
claims arising from an arrangement still require the approvals and evidence
specified in the Expense Reimbursement Policy.

## RW-06: Ownership and review

People Operations owns this policy and reviews it annually with managers and IT
Security. Managers review individual arrangements quarterly for coverage,
performance, and suitability. Changes or withdrawals must be documented and
communicated before taking effect. Employees should consult People Operations
when policy copies appear inconsistent rather than choosing a preferred rule.

Quarterly arrangement reviews should consider service coverage, attendance at
required activities, communication, and the continuing suitability of the home
workspace. Employees may describe practical problems and propose improvements.
People Operations keeps the arrangement decision and subsequent changes
together, so an isolated message cannot be mistaken for the full agreement.
Managers must refer security decisions to IT Security and leave decisions to
the PTO process rather than making informal exceptions outside their authority.
