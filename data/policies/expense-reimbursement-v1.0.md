# Expense Reimbursement Policy v1.0

## EXP-01: Purpose and scope

Northstar Analytics reimburses reasonable, documented business expenses incurred
by employees performing approved company duties. This policy applies to employees
assigned to US offices, including employees with approved remote work
arrangements. All limits are in US dollars. Reimbursement covers actual eligible
spending; an unused limit is not a cash allowance.

Personal purchases, ordinary commuting, alcohol, fines, and expenses reimbursed
by another party are ineligible. Remote work approval alone does not authorize
equipment purchases or reimbursement for household internet, rent, or utilities.
Employees must obtain written approval from their manager and Finance before
buying business equipment.

An expense must have a clear connection to an approved task, trip, or business
activity. Employees should choose practical options and confirm unusual costs
before committing company funds. A meeting invitation, a manager's awareness of
a trip, or an available budget does not by itself establish reimbursement
eligibility. This policy does not change the approvals required for remote
work, authorize work from a new location, or convert personal travel into
business travel. If a trip combines business and personal activities, explain
the business portion and separate its costs before submitting a claim.

## EXP-02: Roles and eligibility

Employees must establish a business purpose, obtain required approvals, and
retain supporting evidence. Managers confirm that purchases serve company work
and fit the team's budget. Finance checks documentation, applies limits, and
processes payment. Employees cannot approve their own claims; a manager's own
expenses require approval from that manager's supervisor.

Reimbursement eligibility depends on the expense and its evidence, rather than
whether the employee worked remotely or onsite. PTO does not create an expense
entitlement. Personal portions of mixed business and personal purchases must be
identified and excluded from the claimed amount.

Employees remain responsible for responding accurately even after a manager
has approved the claim. When a manager is unavailable, ask Finance to identify
an authorized reviewer rather than approving the claim yourself or using a
colleague's account. All approval decisions must remain attached to the claim
so a later reviewer can understand who authorized the expense and why.

## EXP-03: Policy rules

Obtain written manager approval before booking business travel. Apply the limits
and evidence requirements below together. Manager approval does not waive an
expense limit or substitute for Finance review.

### EXP-03.1: Expense limits

EXP-T01: Standard limits for approved business travel

| Expense category | Reimbursement limit in USD | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meals | $75 per employee per calendar day | Actual eligible food and nonalcoholic drinks during business travel, including tax and tips. |
| Lodging | $200 per room per night | Actual room charges, including mandatory fees and taxes; personal incidentals excluded. |
| Ground transport | Actual standard fare | Business travel by taxi, rideshare, or public transit; premium upgrades excluded. |

Meal purchases for the same employee and date share the daily limit, even when
submitted separately. Deduct any meal paid directly by the company from the
available daily limit using its actual cost. Lodging limits apply to each night;
unused amounts from another night cannot offset an excess.

For example, if the company directly pays $25 for an employee's lunch during
approved business travel, no more than $50 remains available for that
employee's other eligible meals on the same date. The employee claims only
actual eligible spending, even when it is below that remaining amount. A
colleague's unused meal limit cannot be transferred to another traveler. For
lodging, compare the eligible charge for each occupied night with the nightly
limit rather than averaging the total stay. Obtain an exception under EXP-05
before booking if the available business accommodation would exceed that
limit. Premium ground transport also requires advance exception review.

### EXP-03.2: Supporting evidence

EXP-T02: Evidence required for each claimed expense

| Expense category | Required evidence | Required business detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meals | Itemized merchant receipt and payment confirmation | Travel purpose, meal date, and employee covered. |
| Lodging | Itemized hotel invoice and payment confirmation | Approved trip purpose and occupied nights. |
| Ground transport | Provider receipt and payment confirmation | Business purpose, travel date, origin, and destination. |

A receipt that also confirms payment satisfies both evidence requirements.
Redact unrelated account information before uploading documents. Store claims
only in the company expense portal, following the Information Security Policy;
do not include passwords or complete payment card numbers.

Evidence should show what was purchased, when it was purchased, the amount,
and whether payment occurred. A bank statement alone does not itemize a meal,
and an unpaid reservation alone does not show a settled hotel charge. If a
single document provides both itemization and payment confirmation, attach it
once and identify the relevant entries. For a shared business purchase, explain
which employee paid and which participants or business activities the cost
covered. Do not submit the same receipt as separate full claims for multiple
employees. Provide a readable explanation of any correction or refund rather
than altering the original receipt. Supporting records are Confidential under
SEC-03.1 and must remain accessible only to authorized reviewers.

## EXP-04: Procedures

Use this process for each claim:

1. Submit the claim through the expense portal within 30 calendar days after the
   expense was incurred. Enter the business purpose, date, category, actual cost,
   eligible amount, and any required advance approval.
2. Attach the evidence listed in EXP-T02 and identify company-paid or personal
   amounts so they are not reimbursed again.
3. The manager reviews the submitted claim within five business days, approving
   it or returning it with a written explanation.
4. Finance reviews an approved, complete claim and issues reimbursement within
   ten business days after receiving it. If information is missing, Finance
   returns the claim; the payment window restarts when a complete, approved
   resubmission is received.

Employees can check claim status in the portal. A returned claim requires a
correction and resubmission rather than a second claim for the same purchase.

Before submission, reconcile the claim against receipts and any company-paid
amounts. Check that the category matches the purchase and that the stated
business purpose explains the work performed. Separate personal additions
such as a private excursion or a meal for a nonbusiness guest. If a receipt
contains both eligible and ineligible items, identify the eligible items and
explain the allocation of related tax or tips. The claim must not include more
than the amount actually paid by the employee for eligible business items.

The manager's review and Finance's payment review are separate steps. The
five-business-day manager review period begins with submission; the
ten-business-day payment period begins only when Finance receives a complete,
approved claim. If Finance returns it for missing evidence, correct the
existing record, attach the requested information, and obtain any required
approval for the revised amount. The portal history should show the reason
for the return and the corrected submission. Employees should contact Finance
if the displayed status is unclear rather than sending repeated copies to
several reviewers.

If a merchant later refunds a reimbursed purchase, notify Finance promptly
through the original claim record. Include the refund amount and related
receipt so Finance can arrange the appropriate correction. Do not offset the
refund against an unrelated future claim without Finance's written direction.

## EXP-05: Exceptions and escalation

Request approval from both the manager and Finance before exceeding a limit or
incurring a category not listed in EXP-T01. Record the approved amount and
business reason. Personal expenses remain ineligible.

For a missing receipt or late submission, provide Finance with a written
explanation and available evidence. Finance must record its decision before
payment. Escalate disputed decisions to the Finance lead; approval is not
automatic.

An exception request must identify the affected expense, expected amount,
reason the ordinary rule cannot be met, and any practical alternatives
considered. Approval applies only to the expense described, not to all future
purchases of the same type. If plans change materially, obtain a revised
decision before incurring the different cost. A missing-receipt explanation
must be truthful and supported by available records; it does not authorize
invented evidence or reimbursement of an otherwise personal expense.

## EXP-06: Ownership and review

Finance owns this policy and reviews limits annually with People Operations.
Managers communicate approved revisions to their teams. Finance retains claims,
approvals, and exception decisions in the expense portal for audit and resolves
questions about duplicate or inconsistent submissions before payment.

Employees may ask Finance to explain a calculation or identify which evidence
is missing. Finance records corrections in the portal so the original claim,
review comments, and final decision remain linked. Managers should use those
records when discussing spending patterns with their teams while limiting
access to personal details. Suspected exposure of claim documents must be
reported through SEC-04 immediately. A payment dispute does not justify
moving receipts to a personal email account or an unapproved storage service.
